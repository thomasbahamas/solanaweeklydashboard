Analysis
Risk is being repriced fast. The total crypto market cap shed 4.8% in 24 hours to $2.62T, BTC dropped to $76,293, and Fear & Greed jumped from 57 to 69 in a single session — a classic divergence where sentiment lags the selloff. The immediate macro catalyst is political: the Clarity Act, which would have provided crypto's first comprehensive regulatory framework, appears dead after Senate Republicans rejected Democratic counter-proposals hours before a key vote. That removes a significant regulatory tailwind that markets had partially priced in. BTC dominance at 58.3% signals the market is in risk-reduction mode, not rotation. When dominance rises during a broad selloff, capital is not rotating into alts — it is retreating to relative safety. SOL at $99.30 sits just below the psychologically critical $100 level, but technically the structure remains intact: price is above both the 50-day MA ($88.95) and 200-day MA ($83.29), with RSI at 48.9 — neutral, not oversold. The setup is not broken, but it needs a catalyst to re-engage. On-chain, the data tells a more interesting story. Solana spot DEX volume surged 41.3% to $2.53B in 24 hours while TVL held essentially flat at $5.84B. This is not panic selling — it is active trading. Fees hit $13.6M on the day. Stablecoins on Solana stand at $65.17B, suggesting dry powder is still parked on-chain. The network is not emptying. The question is whether macro headwinds from the Clarity Act collapse and broader risk-off will overwhelm these on-chain fundamentals.
